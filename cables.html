Wired Cables

The common cables used to connect network devices, with their connectors, distances and typical uses.

🔗

UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair)

Cat5eCat6Cat6a
  • Connector: RJ-45
  • Max length: 100 m
  • Speed: 1 Gbps (Cat5e/Cat6), 10 Gbps (Cat6a)
  • Use: most common LAN cable
🛡️

STP (Shielded Twisted Pair)

  • Connector: RJ-45 (shielded)
  • Max length: 100 m
  • Speed: up to 10 Gbps
  • Use: places with a lot of electrical interference
📺

Coaxial

  • Connector: BNC (old Ethernet), F-type (cable TV/internet)
  • Max length: about 185 to 500 m (legacy Ethernet)
  • Speed: 10 Mbps legacy; higher on cable internet
  • Use: cable TV, cable broadband, old bus networks
💡

Fiber optic: single-mode

  • Connector: LC, SC
  • Max length: 10 km to 40 km or more
  • Speed: 10 Gbps to 100 Gbps and beyond
  • Use: ISP backbones, long-distance links
🌈

Fiber optic: multi-mode

  • Connector: LC, SC, ST
  • Max length: about 300 to 550 m at 10 Gbps
  • Speed: 10 Gbps to 100 Gbps
  • Use: campus and data-center links
🧑‍💻

Console (rollover) cable

  • Connector: RJ-45 to DB-9 or USB
  • Length: short (a few metres)
  • Typically light blue
  • Use: command-line setup of routers and switches
⏱️

Serial cable (DCE / DTE)

  • Connector: V.35, DB-60 or smart serial
  • Speed: up to about 1.544 Mbps (T1)
  • The DCE side supplies the clock rate
  • Use: WAN links between routers
➡️

Straight-through

  • Same wiring standard on both ends (T568B to T568B)
  • Connects different device types
  • Examples: PC to switch, switch to router
🔀

Crossover

  • T568A on one end, T568B on the other
  • Connects same device types
  • Examples: PC to PC, switch to switch
  • Modern devices with Auto-MDIX detect this automatically
